## 1. THE REGULATORY BASELINE: LETHAL AUTONOMOUS WEAPON SYSTEMS AND DODD 3000.09

### A. The Statutory Hurdle: Verification, Validation, and the Ambiguity of Human Control
The primary framework governing autonomous systems within the U.S. military is Department of Defense Directive (DoDD) 3000.09, "Autonomy in Weapon Systems" (updated January 25, 2023). The directive establishes policy and responsibilities for developing and using autonomous and semi-autonomous functions in weapon systems, prioritizing the minimization of failures that could lead to unintended engagements.

DoDD 3000.09 does not prohibit Lethal Autonomous Weapon Systems (LAWS). Instead, it defines LAWS as systems that, once activated, can select and engage targets without further intervention by a human operator ("human-out-of-the-loop"). The directive imposes an exceptionally high bar for compliance, mandating that systems undergo rigorous hardware and software Verification and Validation (V&V), alongside realistic Testing and Evaluation (T&E) that simulates adaptive adversaries and practicable countermeasures.

Crucially, policy dictates that autonomous systems must allow commanders and operators to exercise "appropriate levels of human judgment over the use of force." The term "appropriate" is context-dependent, varying across operational domains. Furthermore, the directive classifies "semi-autonomous" weapons as those intended to engage only individual targets or specific target groups selected by a human operator—a broad category encompassing "fire-and-forget" and lock-on-after-launch mechanisms that bypass the formal Senior Review process required for fully autonomous systems.

Formal development and fielding of fully autonomous systems require approval from the Under Secretary of Defense for Policy, the Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, and the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment. While the Deputy Secretary of Defense may waive this Senior Review for "urgent military need," Congress enacted Section 1061 of the FY2026 National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) to mandate that the DoD notify congressional defense committees within 30 days of any such waiver, providing an unclassified explanation of the technology and rationale.

In multi-domain operations—such as naval hybrid fleets utilizing the doctrine of "command by negation"—autonomous nodes execute actions unless explicitly countermanded by a human operator. For probabilistic AI systems, proving strict V&V to satisfy these doctrines is nearly impossible because their outputs are inherently statistical, making it impossible to guarantee that a model will not exceed its authorized logic envelope under chaotic combat conditions.
